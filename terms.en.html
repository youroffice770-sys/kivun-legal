In short

Kivun is a digital calendar and planner for Chabad emissaries. It is used through a yearly subscription bought inside the app, on the App Store or on Google Play. The subscription renews automatically, and you can cancel it at any time through the store. Your information is kept on your own device.

One thing worth reading to the end: the times and dates the app shows are a planning aid, not a halachic ruling. The "Times and dates" section below explains exactly why.

What the subscription includes

  • A full Hebrew year, with holidays, new months, fasts and Chabad dates
  • The entry and exit times for your location: candle lighting, the end of Shabbat and the end of a fast
  • Five planning pages: yearly, monthly, event, fundraising and review
  • Daily tasks and local reminders
  • Scheduling items from the planning pages into the calendar
  • Adding the whole year to the device's calendar, and exporting the calendar to a file
  • Backup and restore of your data to a file

After the initial setup, everything works without an internet connection.

The free week

Anyone opening a subscription for the first time gets seven days free. At the end of the seven days the charge is made automatically, unless the subscription was cancelled before then. You can cancel at any point during the week, and then nothing is charged.

What the free week does not include: exporting the whole year to a calendar, meaning adding the year to the device's calendar and exporting the calendar to a file. Both of those arrive with the full subscription. Everything else, including the calendar, the times, the planning, the tasks, the reminders and the backup, is fully open during the free week.

The free week is offered once per store account, under Apple's and Google's own rules. If that account has subscribed before, billing starts immediately.

What happens when the subscription ends

The app locks, and your information stays on the device. The backup button is available on the lock screen too, so you can always take your data out to a file. Renewing brings everything back exactly as it was.

Times and dates: a planning aid, not a halachic ruling

The times the app shows, including candle lighting, the end of Shabbat, the end of a fast and the daily zmanim, are calculated on the device from the location you entered, using accepted calculation methods. They may differ from your community's own calendar and from local custom.

The calculation depends on several things outside our control: how precise the chosen location is, the device's clock and time zone, and, in Israel, the number of minutes before sunset that the local custom lights, which varies by city and can be changed in the settings.

For a practical question, rely on your local calendar and on your own rav, not on the app. The Chabad dates, the weekly portions and the holidays shown in the app are likewise information for planning. The app issues no halachic ruling and is no substitute for asking a rav.

We correct inaccuracies when they come to light, and it is not possible to undertake that the information will be accurate in every place and every situation.
